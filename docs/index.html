The missing middle

Canada has thousands of open datasets. It does not yet have a common way for machines to understand how they relate.

Portals publish files. Search engines index pages. AI systems guess at the connections. Open Reference adds the durable identifiers, vocabulary mappings, reconciliations and provenance needed to turn fragmented releases into dependable public knowledge—without taking control away from the people who publish it.

Our public-interest commitments

Private delivery. Public value.

Open Reference earns revenue by building and operating reliable services—not by enclosing public data.

01

Open by default

Core catalogue metadata, mappings, provenance and eligible derived data remain openly reusable.

02

Authority stays at source

Open Reference does not rewrite public facts. Publishers remain authoritative and every transformation points back to them.

03

Built for exit

Open standards, portable exports and non-exclusive relationships prevent public infrastructure from becoming a trap.

04

Human-accountable

Automation helps at scale; validation rules, release notes and visible provenance make the result inspectable.

Built with the ecosystem

Open data is a community practice, not a software product.

Open Reference works with governments, libraries, universities, civil society, Indigenous data-governance leaders, sector organizations and open-data practitioners. Community partners define priorities and evaluate public value. Open Reference turns those priorities into maintained infrastructure.
